# Casting Spells
Casting a spell requires two key factors from the caster (the combatant performing the spell):
- Their cognitive focus, represented by either an Intelligence or Charisma Skill Check roll. Each spell will specify which it its description.
- An energy cost, or a reaction cost.

Additionally, each spell has an associated risk, usually in the form of a [[Stress]] penalty, that burdens the caster on a failed cast.

Spells are divided into three main categories, each of which has slight differences that are of note (other than quick spells):
## Quick Spells
Quick Spells are the most basic form of spells, they follow all rules previously established. Despite their name, their energy costs can still range within the usual range.
## Held Spells
Held Spells are spells that are *held* over turns and usually have a continuous effect. Held spells differ from quick spells in the following ways:
- Only one Held Spell can be in use at once. Casting a Held Spell while another Held Spell is active will replace the active Held Spell.
- When the caster of a Held Spell loses HP (*not* taking damage, see [[Block & HP]]), the caster must re-roll the Spell's DC roll. If the roll fails, then the Held Spell stops, stops providing its effects, and counts as a failed spell cast burdening the caster with the spell's associated risk. If the roll succeeds, the spell continues as normal.
- Held Spells can be stopped a.k.a. "*dispelled*" with a reaction *during your turn*.
## Attack Spells
Some spells will be denoted as "Attack Spells", these spells function slightly different from quick spells in the following ways:
- Attack Spells do not list a required DC Skill Check amount in order to succeed the casting. Instead, the DC amount required is the target(s) [[Hit Chance]].
	- For example, if a player wanted to cast a Biology Attack Spell against a target with a [[Hit Chance]] of 13, the player would need to roll at or above a 13.
	- If there are multiple targets, use the median DC amount (rounded down if there are an even amount of targets.)
- Attack Spells count as Attack Rolls.
# Burning Spells
As mentioned in the [[Spells#Spell Slots|Spell Slots section]], each known spell has a corresponding slot. When a spell is "burned", that slot, and thus that spell, becomes temporarily unusable. All burned spells are restored after resting for a minimum of 8 hours.

Many effects require or cause the burning of spells, however, it is not the only way. For 1 [[Energy]], any combatant can choose to burn one of their spell in order to gain advantage on their next Skill Check. Note that advantage is _not_ gained from other effects causing the burning of spells, only manual spell burning. This effect does not stack, in this instance, all advantage is used on the next  Skill Check.
